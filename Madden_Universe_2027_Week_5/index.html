FILM RECAP / BROWNS AT RAVENS

A DROP. AN ANSWER.
A NUMBER THAT STAYS.

Nasir moved too quickly on a comeback route, turning toward daylight before the ball was secure. He did not hide from it. The next stretch told the story: an immediate answer on a sharp post, a devastating out breaking release with no defender close, a spectacular diving catch, and a short crossing touchdown.

But Nasir’s favorite highlight was not one of his own. He wanted reporters to study Caleb Williams escaping the pocket late in the fourth and delivering a dime to Carroll. Williams finished with 442 passing yards, six passing touchdowns and zero interceptions. Meanwhile, Cleveland’s J. Storm threw for 486 yards and six touchdowns but the Browns committed four turnovers, enough to turn a massive offensive day into a seven point loss.

FILM RECAP / TENNESSEE AT JACKSONVILLE

THE RED ZONE HAD ONE ANSWER.

First came an aggressive cornerback blitz, with Knox arriving to force an incompletion. Then came the red zone sequence: Jacksonville tried to feed Chase, returned to the same matchup, and tried it again. Three attempts failed. After all that, the Jaguars tested him a fourth time. Same matchup. Same result. Fort Knox. Another stop.

Chase’s 3 catches for 81 yards still reflected his danger, and Knox openly credited him. The more revealing detail is where those yards came from: 37 yards against Knox in the matchup account, no touchdowns and four failed red zone challenges. Knox was credited with one solo tackle and three passes defended for the game. The entire Jacksonville offense finished with 92 offensive yards, zero rushing yards and four turnovers.

THE ROOKIE COULD BARELY BREATHE.

Reporter (opening)

Thirty five to nothing, Jacksonville with just 92 offensive yards, four turnovers, and Ja’Marr Chase held out of the end zone. What was the plan?

Lachlan Knox (relaxed)

There isn’t some magic trick, mate. We talked, we trusted our calls, and we did our jobs. Chase is ridiculous. You don’t go into that matchup thinking you’ve solved him.

Reporter (curious)

They went back toward Chase four times in that red zone sequence, including three straight. Were you surprised?

Lachlan Knox (laughing)

Honestly? I was knackered. Wanted to come off for a breather. They kept lining up the same way, coach kept pointing at me to stay, so I stayed. I was thinking, “Surely they’re not throwing another one?” Then they threw another one.

Reporter (teasing)

You were breathing pretty hard after that fourth attempt.

Lachlan Knox (grinning)

I’m not going to pretend I wasn’t. That bloke had me working. But I’d rather be tired with the ball hitting the ground than comfortable watching six points go up.

Reporter (fact check)

Chase still finished with 81 yards. Does that undercut the headline?

Lachlan Knox (matter of fact)

No. He earned his catches. Only 37 of those yards came when I had him. He’s one of the best on the planet. You don’t disrespect a player like that because you won one Sunday.

Reporter (final)

Did you call it Fort Knox out there?

Lachlan Knox (dry smile)

I didn’t have enough oxygen left to call it anything.

RECORD THE RESULT, NOT A MYTH: Chase was not held catchless. Knox did not intercept him. He won a decisive stretch of coverage and respected a receiver who still produced 81 yards.

GAME THREE / GREEN BAY 34 / MINNESOTA 31

GIBBS IS BACK.
NOW WHERE’S THE QB?

Green Bay went into Minnesota facing Joe Burrow, Saquon Barkley and an intimidating roster. They left with a fifth consecutive win. Gibbs needed one afternoon to make up for three games of inactivity. Ryan Miller needed only eleven pass attempts.

PREGAME / NO MICROPHONE

HE WALKED IN.
THAT WAS IT.

Miller arrived without an interview. He had thrown six touchdowns and just one interception across the first four weeks, earning a reputation as a steady replacement for the injured Kyler Murray. But with Jahmyr Gibbs available, the entire offense could shift.

Against Minnesota’s roster, the question was whether Green Bay could control the tempo well enough to avoid a pass heavy shootout with Burrow.

GAME RECAP / GREEN BAY AT MINNESOTA

THE RUN GAME WAS THE WHOLE GAME PLAN.

Gibbs erupted for 25 carries, 239 rushing yards and three rushing touchdowns, then added five catches, 81 receiving yards and another score. Four total touchdowns, 320 scrimmage yards, and an NFC Offensive Player of the Week award were his return statement. The projected Saquon versus Gibbs showdown ended with both backs productive, but Gibbs made the biggest difference.

Ryan Miller completed 8 of 11 passes for 119 yards, one touchdown and one interception. He was sacked five times. It was his lightest passing workload of the year. Green Bay won 34 to 31 anyway, even as Burrow threw for 283 yards and Minnesota amassed 421 offensive yards. The Vikings’ six interceptions made the difference between a stacked lineup and a winning one.

What does this mean for Miller? It does not erase his previous games, including the wins with Gibbs out. But for a quarterback trying to build a reputation, winning this way creates a new argument: game manager or passenger?
