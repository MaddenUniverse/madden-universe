GAME RECAP / SEA VS BAL

THE TEAM DID NOT STOP.
THE ROOKIE DID.

Caleb Williams completes 15 of 22 passes for 193 yards, three touchdowns and zero interceptions. Baltimore runs for 201 yards and wins 35 to 7.

Alec Pierce catches six passes for 106 yards and a touchdown, while M. Carroll makes two touchdown catches on 31 receiving yards. The receiving production spreads around with Nasir unavailable.

Seattle's Sam Darnold finishes 22 of 38 for 186 yards and is sacked six times. Seattle's Jake Webster catches nine passes for 121 yards, but the Seahawks manage just one touchdown.

THE DISCUSSION: A week ago analysts questioned whether Baltimore relied too heavily on Cruz. In Week 6, the Ravens answered by winning without him. That says something about the roster, not that the rookie is expendable.

GAME RECAP / DET VS GB

GIBBS FINISHES IT.
DETROIT MOVES THE BALL.

Ryan Miller finishes 8 of 15 for 155 yards, zero passing touchdowns and one interception, with two sacks taken and a 61.8 passer rating. He adds a 20 yard run. Green Bay wins 31 to 27.

Jahmyr Gibbs rushes 13 times for 122 yards and three touchdowns, averaging 9.4 yards per carry. Detroit quarterback Arthur King throws for 425 yards and four touchdowns, with one interception, as Detroit piles up 463 offensive yards to Green Bay's 279.

The Packers do not need Miller to win a passing duel. But if this offense plans to compete through January, the central question remains: what happens when a defense takes Gibbs away?

FAIR CONTEXT: Miller has shown he can produce passing touchdowns without Gibbs. The recent two game sample raises a legitimate development question, but it does not prove the rookie cannot throw.

THE WINS DO NOT
END THE ARGUMENT.

Baltimore won without its record setting rookie, Tennessee put up a second consecutive zero, and Green Bay survived a 425 yard passing performance by Detroit. The First Take panel has plenty to disagree about.

ESPN

FIRST TAKE / THE AFTERMATH

Shae Cornette (opening)

All three of our teams won. Baltimore beats Seattle 35 to 7, Tennessee shuts out Carolina 33 to nothing, and Green Bay slips past Detroit 31 to 27. Stephen A., which result are we overlooking?

Stephen A. Smith (raising voice)

Can we talk about Ryan Miller? EIGHT completions! Fifteen attempts! No touchdowns, an interception! And everybody tells me the Packers WON, like that is supposed to close the case. Gibbs scored three touchdowns and Detroit threw for 425 yards!

Dan Orlovsky (analytical)

I hear the frustration, but you cannot punish a quarterback for having a great running back. Gibbs averages 9.4 yards per carry. You keep giving him the ball. The interception is worth studying, sure, but fifteen attempts can also be a coaching decision.

Ryan Clark (challenging)

Dan, then answer this: what does Ryan Miller look like when Green Bay is down fourteen and Gibbs is getting two yards a carry? I am not saying the kid is a bust. I am saying his ceiling is still an unanswered question.

Marcus Spears (animated)

While y’all interrogate a winning quarterback, TENNESSEE JUST SHUT SOMEBODY OUT AGAIN! That is two straight games! Mitchell picked off three passes. Knox got five tackles. We ought to be talking about this entire defense.

Shae Cornette (concerned)

We also have to talk about Nasir Cruz. After his 316 yard game, the rookie suffers a concussion, plays only a few snaps and is held out. We are not speculating about when he will be back.

Ryan Clark (firm)

Exactly. Forget the fantasy numbers. If it is a concussion, the player has to recover and be cleared. Credit Baltimore for continuing to execute without him, but it does not make his health any less important.

Stephen A. Smith (conceding)

I can agree with that. And yes, Baltimore has other weapons. Caleb had three touchdowns, Pierce had over a hundred, Carroll scored twice. We learned this is a TEAM, not just one sensational rookie.

Shae Cornette (closing)

Tennessee looks terrifying, Baltimore looks deeper, and Green Bay remains the most debated winning team on this show. That is Week 6.
